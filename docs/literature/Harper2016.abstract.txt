Sequence variation in two genes determines the efficacy of transmission of 
citrus tristeza virus by the brown citrus aphid.

Harper SJ(1), Killiny N(2), Tatineni S(3), Gowda S(2), Cowell SJ(2), Shilts 
T(2), Dawson WO(2).

Vector transmission is an important part of the viral infection cycle, yet for 
many viruses little is known about this process, or how viral sequence variation 
affects transmission efficacy. Here we examined the effect of substituting genes 
from the highly transmissible FS577 isolate of citrus tristeza virus (CTV) in to 
the poorly transmissible T36-based infectious clone. We found that introducing 
p65 or p61 sequences from FS577 significantly increased transmission efficacy. 
Interestingly, replacement of both genes produced a greater increase than either 
gene alone, suggesting that CTV transmission requires the concerted action of 
co-evolved p65 and p61 proteins.
