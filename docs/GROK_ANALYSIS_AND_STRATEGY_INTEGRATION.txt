WHAT GROK PROPOSES (Vector-Incompatibility Approach)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Instead of the approach I outlined (disable transmission by reversing K174R in p33),
Grok's document proposes the RECIPROCAL strategy:

  "Engineer FS577 (24.1% transmission) to RESEMBLE T36 in p65+p61,
   leaving p27 intact, and measure whether transmission DROPS."

This is operationally clever for several reasons:

  1. FS577 is COMPATIBLE with the vector (it transmits well at baseline)
     → We know we're not breaking the fundamental virion
  
  2. It keeps p27 unchanged (known to be high-transmission capable)
     → Isolates p65+p61 as causal factors (if transmission drops)
  
  3. It's a POSITIVE SWAP (adding T36-like residues) not a negative knockout
     → May be easier to achieve virion assembly/stability
  
  4. It directly tests Harper's finding: p65+p61 account for ~18 percentage
     points of transmission gain (T36→FS577 backdrop)

KEY DIFFERENCE FROM MY HYPOTHESIS
─────────────────────────────────

I proposed:
  - Start with T36 (low transmission)
  - Change K174R in p33 → expect transmission ↓ to <5%
  - Then add p65+p61 mutations
  - Test reciprocal swap second

Grok proposes:
  - Start with FS577 (high transmission)
  - Change p65+p61 to match T36 → expect transmission ↓ significantly
  - If that works, investigate p33 in Phase 2
  - Don't need reciprocal swap if phenotype reverses

WHICH APPROACH IS STRONGER?
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

GROK'S STRENGTHS:
  ✓ Tests the MAJOR effect first (p65+p61 = ~18 points vs p33 = ~5–7 points)
  ✓ Starts with a background that transmits (less risk of viral fitness loss)
  ✓ Doesn't require explaining away K174R (leaves p33 untouched initially)
  ✓ Operationally simpler (6–9 mutations vs. 3, and p27 stays locked)
  ✓ Gives a CONTAINMENT design if it works (FS577 with broken p65/p61)
  ✓ Aligns with Harper's most confident finding (p65/p61 together)

MY APPROACH'S STRENGTHS:
  ✓ Tests the mechanistically ISOLATED change (K174R in p33)
  ✓ Directly addresses the "what makes high transmitters special?" question
  ✓ p33 is involved in multiple functions (replication, silencing suppression)
    → Understanding its role is high-value for the field
  ✗ Requires K174R to be the sole p33 change (more assumptions)
  ✗ Doesn't explain why K174R matters (mechanism unknown)
  ✗ Higher risk: T36 is already crippled; further mutation may break it

VERDICT

Grok's strategy is OPERATIONALLY SUPERIOR for the near term (4–6 weeks):

WHY THIS CHANGES MY HYPOTHESIS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

My hypothesis stated:
  "The transmission phenotype of CTV is controlled by three specific amino acid 
   changes in p33 (including K174R) and by cooperating changes in p65 and p61."

That's not wrong, but it puts p33 too early. The evidence actually says:

  REVISED HYPOTHESIS:
  "The transmission phenotype of CTV is controlled primarily by changes in p65 
   and p61 (accounting for ~18 percentage points of the FS577→T36 difference).
   p33 contributes an additional ~5–7 points, with K174R as a mechanistically 
   isolated candidate. These can be individually identified, tested in combination,
   and validated in reciprocal backgrounds."

The change:
  - Leads with p65+p61 (the bigger effect, better-supported in Harper 2016)
  - Positions p33 as Phase 2 (still testable, but not the rate-limiting step)
  - Keeps the reciprocal-swap validation as a *confirming* step, not a 
    primary design driver
